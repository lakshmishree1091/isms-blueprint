## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the parties with an interest in Meridiaan's information security, records what each of them requires, and notes where that requirement comes from. It builds on the context in `01-company-profile.md` and feeds the ISMS scope (`03-scope-statement.md`) and the risk assessment. ISO/IEC 27001 clause 4.2 also asks which of these requirements will be addressed through the ISMS; unless noted, all requirements listed here are in scope of the ISMS.

## 2. Interested parties and their requirements

Requirement **type** is marked as legal (from law or regulation), contractual (from a client or supplier agreement), or business (a commercial or organisational expectation).

| Interested party | What they need from Meridiaan | Type | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owners and executive management | The business, its reputation and its ability to keep serving clients protected; an effective ISMS that justifies the resources spent on it; regulatory duties met so the company is not fined or dropped from client supplier registers | Business + Legal | Board mandate and strategy; NIS2 management-body accountability |
| Employees and staff | A safe and lawful workplace; clear, workable security rules and training; sound handling of shift work and on-call; their own personal data handled properly | Legal + Business | Belgian employment law; GDPR; internal security policy |
| Financial-sector clients (Client A, a bank; Client B, an insurer) | Strong security and operational resilience for the services they rely on; contractual audit and inspection rights; incident notification within tight agreed deadlines; documented exit and continuity plans; evidence they can show their own supervisor; support for their DORA register of information | Contractual | Client contracts, driven by DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) |
| Other clients (manufacturing, logistics and retail, healthcare, public sector) | Reliable and secure services; protection of their data; increasingly, proof of good practice such as an ISO/IEC 27001 certificate before signing or renewing; NIS2 supply-chain requirements passed down by clients who are themselves in scope | Contractual + Business | Client contracts; tenders and due-diligence questionnaires |
| Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium (CCB), the NIS2 authority | Registration as an important entity; appropriate cybersecurity risk-management measures; incident reporting within the statutory deadlines; cooperation with supervision | Legal | Belgian NIS2 Act, transposing Directive (EU) 2022/2555 |
| National Bank of Belgium (NBB), indirectly | Meridiaan is not directly supervised, but the NBB supervises Clients A and B, so its expectations reach Meridiaan through those clients' third-party risk management: audit rights, resilience-testing support and oversight of the outsourcing chain | Contractual (indirect) | DORA and NBB outsourcing/ICT expectations, flowed down through client contracts |
| Belgian Data Protection Authority (GBA/APD) | Lawful processing of personal data; Meridiaan to meet its obligations as a processor for client data and controller for its own; breach notification where required | Legal | GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and Belgian data-protection law |
| Colocation facility operators (critical suppliers) | A two-way relationship: Meridiaan needs reliable power, cooling, physical security and access control from them, plus assurance evidence it can pass to clients; they in turn require Meridiaan to follow the facility's access and safety rules | Contractual + Business | Supplier contracts and SLAs; physical-security assurance requirements |
| Other suppliers (connectivity, energy, equipment, sub-processors) | Reliable supply and appropriate security from them; and they expect Meridiaan to honour its contract terms | Contractual + Business | Supplier contracts and SLAs |
| Data subjects (employees, client contacts, prospects) | Their personal data kept confidential, accurate and available, and their data-protection rights respected | Legal | GDPR |

## 3. Climate change note

ISO/IEC 27001 Amendment 1:2024 records that relevant interested parties can have requirements related to climate change. For Meridiaan this appears mainly as client and regulator expectations of resilience against climate-driven disruption to power and hosting sites. This links to the climate-change determination in `01-company-profile.md` and is carried into continuity planning and the risk register.

## 4. Assumptions

- The list reflects the company as described in `01-company-profile.md`; it is reviewed when clients, suppliers, contracts or regulation change.
- Specific contractual figures such as notification deadlines and audit frequencies are set out per client in the supplier-management and incident-response material, not here.
