## 1. Purpose
This is a one-page summary of Meridiaan's information security position for leadership. It draws on the full programme in this repository: the company context, risk register, control set, gap assessment and regulatory analysis. Read this first; the numbered folders provide the detail behind each conclusion.

## 2. Where we stand
Meridiaan is a managed IT and hosting provider whose business depends on protecting its clients' systems and data — including two financial-sector clients who rely on us for services they treat as critical. Security is therefore not just good practice for us; it is a legal obligation and a condition of doing business.

We have built an information security management system (ISMS) aligned to the international standard ISO/IEC 27001. The honest headline is this: **we already do most of the right things technically, but we cannot yet consistently prove it.** Our technical defences are relatively strong; our documentation, evidence and formal governance are still catching up. This is normal for a company that has grown quickly, and it is exactly what this programme is designed to close.

## 3. Our biggest risks
Assessed as things stand today, our sharpest exposures come from the ways an attacker could get in and then reach many clients at once:
- **Compromise of our privileged access or management tools** — because we hold the "keys" to many client systems, one breach could affect several clients simultaneously.
- **Phishing and unpatched systems** — the most common entry routes, and the cheapest for an attacker to try.
- **A failure to recover, or to report an incident on time** — which would breach commitments to our financial clients and to regulators.

None of these is unusual for our type of business; all are being actively addressed.

## 4. Our regulatory position
- **NIS2** (EU cybersecurity law): we are directly in scope as an important entity. Our ISO 27001 programme meets all ten of the law's required security measures; the outstanding work is formal registration, incident-reporting readiness and governance.
- **DORA** (financial-sector resilience law): this reaches us through our banking and insurance clients' contracts, not directly. Our programme covers most of what they need to see; the remainder is specific contract terms, records and testing support we are putting in place.
- **GDPR**: we meet our duties as a data processor and controller, with improvements underway in evidence and formal review.

The efficient insight behind our approach: **one well-built ISO 27001 system satisfies the large majority of all three regimes at once**, so we comply once rather than three times — with a defined set of legal "add-ons" on top.

## 5. What we recommend
A phased plan, prioritised by risk:
- **Now (0–3 months):** lock down privileged access, enforce multi-factor authentication everywhere, set a firm deadline for critical security patches, and finalise our incident-reporting playbook against NIS2 and client deadlines.
- **Next (3–9 months):** formalise supplier assurance and our financial-client obligations, prove our backup and recovery actually work, and complete data-separation and monitoring controls.
- **Then (9–18 months):** complete documentation, run an internal audit and management review, and prepare for an ISO/IEC 27001 certification decision — the single strongest signal we can give clients and regulators.

## 6. The bottom line
Meridiaan's security is in reasonable shape and improving on a clear, risk-driven path. The priority is to turn strong practice into provable, certified assurance — protecting our clients, meeting our legal duties, and safeguarding the trust our business depends on.
